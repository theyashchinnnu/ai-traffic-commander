# Emergency Response Standard Operating Procedures (SOP)

## 1. Multi-Vehicle Accident Response Protocol
- **Phase 1: Initial Notification & Dispatch**:
  - Upon receiving an incident report, the Traffic Control Center must log: exact location (via geocoding), estimated number of vehicles involved, and presence of injuries or fire.
  - Dispatch emergency services (Ambulance, Fire Tender, Highway Patrol) within 90 seconds of log entry.
- **Phase 2: Scene Containment & Safety Perimeter**:
  - The first arriving highway patrol unit must position the patrol vehicle 50 meters behind the accident scene, angled across lanes with emergency beacons active to deflect incoming traffic.
  - Deploy high-visibility traffic cones to establish a taper zone:
    - Speed limit < 50 km/h: 30-meter taper.
    - Speed limit > 80 km/h: 100-meter taper.
  - Place retroreflective warning triangles 100 meters upstream of the containment zone.
- **Phase 3: Victim Extraction and Triage**:
  - Medical personnel must perform rapid triage using the START (Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment) method.
  - Establish a designated triage and treatment area outside the active roadway footprint.
- **Phase 4: Scene Clearance & Traffic Restoration**:
  - Tow trucks must be called immediately for disabled vehicles.
  - Clean up debris and road spills (oil, coolant) using sand or absorbent materials before opening lanes.

## 2. Hazardous Material (Hazmat) Spill Procedures
- **Identification & Hazard Assessment**:
  - Emergency responders must identify the hazardous material using the UN number displayed on the vehicle's orange placard.
  - Reference the Emergency Response Guidebook (ERG) to determine initial isolation and protective action distances.
- **Isolation Zones**:
  - Establish an immediate isolation zone of at least 100 meters in all directions.
  - If a gas or volatile liquid is leaking, establish a downwind protective action zone (typically 500 meters to 1.5 kilometers depending on material toxicity).
- **Control and Containment**:
  - Fire services must use appropriate foam or water fog to suppress vapors, ensuring run-off does not enter municipal storm drains or waterways.
  - Coordinate with specialized environmental cleanup agencies to neutralize and remove the spilled substance.
- **Traffic Diversions for Hazmat**:
  - Complete closure of the affected road section is mandatory.
  - All civilian traffic must be diverted at least 2 kilometers upstream of the isolation perimeter.

## 3. Hit-and-Run Response Protocols
- **Information Gathering**:
  - Capture witness testimonies, vehicle descriptions, license plate fragments, and direction of flight.
  - Access traffic surveillance cameras near the incident site to trace the suspect vehicle.
- **Alert Dissemination**:
  - Issue an immediate "All Points Bulletin" (APB) to all patrol units in the sector.
  - Update toll plazas and check posts along the flight path to monitor for the suspect vehicle.
- **Evidence Preservation**:
  - Secure the collision site to preserve paint scrapes, glass fragments, tire skid marks, and vehicle parts left at the scene.
  - Document the scene with high-resolution photography and coordinate with forensic analysts.

## 4. Emergency Lane Clearance (Green Corridor SOP)
- **Concept & Objective**:
  - A Green Corridor is a dedicated, traffic-free route cleared dynamically by traffic police to facilitate the rapid transit of critical medical cargo, organ transplants, or severely injured patients.
- **Coordination & Communication**:
  - The dispatching hospital must request a Green Corridor from the Traffic Control Center at least 30 minutes prior to transport, providing the route, vehicle registration, and departure time.
  - Establish a dedicated radio frequency for all pilot vehicles and intersection officers.
- **Execution Strategy**:
  - Deploy pilot police outriders 200 meters ahead of the emergency vehicle to clear lane blockages.
  - Traffic signals along the corridor must be manually overridden to show continuous green phases as the emergency vehicle approaches.
  - Intersection officers must physically block cross-traffic to prevent unauthorized entries.

## 5. Agency Roles and Chain of Command
- **Incident Commander (IC)**:
  - The senior traffic police officer at the scene assumes the role of Incident Commander. The IC has final authority over traffic flow, road closures, and diversions.
- **Medical Response Team Leader**:
  - Responsible for patient triage, treatment, and medical transport prioritization. Coordinates directly with receiving hospitals.
- **Fire Service Captain**:
  - Responsible for fire suppression, search and rescue, hazmat containment, and extraction of trapped occupants.
- **Road Maintenance Agency (NHAI/Municipal)**:
  - Responsible for debris clearance, repairing damaged crash barriers, replacing traffic signals, and restoring road surface integrity.
