# Indian Traffic Rules & Regulations Knowledge Base

## 1. General Principles of Lane Discipline
- **Keep Left Rule**: Every vehicle operator on a two-way street must keep their vehicle to the left-hand side of the road, except when overtaking another vehicle or when signage indicates otherwise.
- **Overtaking Protocols**: 
  - Overtaking must always be done from the right side of the vehicle being overtaken.
  - Overtaking from the left is strictly prohibited, unless the vehicle ahead has signaled an intention to turn right and has moved to the center of the road.
  - Never attempt to overtake on curves, bridges, pedestrian crossings, or narrow sections of road.
- **Lane Markings and Compliance**:
  - **Solid White Line**: Indicates that lane changing is prohibited. Vehicles must maintain their current lane.
  - **Broken White Line**: Indicates that lane changing is permitted with caution and proper signaling.
  - **Double Solid Yellow Lines**: Strictly prohibits crossing or straddling the lines for overtaking or U-turns under any circumstances.

## 2. Speed Limits by Vehicle and Road Category
- **Expressways and National Highways**:
  - Private passenger cars (M1 category): Maximum speed limit is 120 km/h on expressways and 100 km/h on national highways.
  - Commercial buses and goods vehicles: Maximum speed limit is 100 km/h on expressways and 80 km/h on national highways.
  - Two-wheelers: Maximum speed limit is 80 km/h on highways; entry is generally prohibited on expressways.
- **Arterial and Urban Roads**:
  - All motor vehicles: Maximum speed limit is 50 km/h, unless local municipal signage specifies a lower limit.
- **Residential Zones, Commercial Districts, and Institutional Areas**:
  - Maximum speed limit for all vehicles is restricted to 30 km/h.
- **School Zones and Hospital Zones**:
  - Maximum speed limit is restricted to 20 km/h. High-visibility warning signs must be installed 50 meters prior to the zone entrance.

## 3. Right of Way and Intersection Control
- **Uncontrolled Intersections**:
  - When two vehicles approach an intersection from different roads at approximately the same time, the vehicle on the left must yield the right of way to the vehicle on the right.
- **Rotaries and Roundabouts**:
  - Vehicles already inside the roundabout traffic circle have absolute right of way. Vehicles entering the roundabout must yield to traffic approaching from their right.
- **Emergency Vehicle Priority**:
  - All motorists must immediately yield the right of way to emergency vehicles (ambulances, fire tenders, police response units) showing active visual and auditory signals.
  - Drivers should pull over safely to the left edge of the road to create a clear passage corridor in the center.

## 4. Pedestrian Rights and Safety Regulations
- **Pedestrian Crossings (Zebra Crossings)**:
  - Pedestrians have absolute right of way at unsignalized pedestrian crossings.
  - Drivers approaching a zebra crossing must slow down and, if necessary, stop to allow pedestrians to cross.
- **Footpaths and Sidewalks**:
  - Motorized vehicles, including two-wheelers, are strictly prohibited from driving or parking on footpaths and sidewalks.
- **School Zone Safeguards**:
  - Drivers must exercise extreme caution in school zones, yielding to children crossing the street even outside designated zebra crossings.

## 5. Penalties and Enforcement Framework (Motor Vehicles Act)
- **Drunken Driving**:
  - Blood Alcohol Concentration (BAC) limit is 30 mg per 100 ml of blood.
  - First offense: Fine up to ₹10,000 and/or imprisonment up to 6 months.
- **Over-Speeding**:
  - Light motor vehicles: Fine ranging from ₹1,000 to ₹2,000.
  - Medium/Heavy passenger or goods vehicles: Fine ranging from ₹2,000 to ₹4,000, with suspension of driving license for subsequent offenses.
- **Dangerous Driving**:
  - Fine up to ₹5,000 and/or imprisonment up to 1 year, along with license suspension.
- **Use of Handheld Mobile Devices while Driving**:
  - Fine up to ₹5,000 and license suspension for up to 3 months. Only hands-free navigation devices mounted securely on the dashboard are permitted.
- **Failure to Yield to Emergency Vehicles**:
  - Fine up to ₹10,000 and/or imprisonment up to 6 months.

## 6. Parking Restrictions and Tow-away Zones
- **Intersections and Crossings**:
  - Parking is strictly prohibited within 15 meters of any road intersection or corner.
- **Public Utility Stops**:
  - No parking within 10 meters of a bus stop, taxi stand, or metro station entrance.
- **Hydrants and Critical Access Points**:
  - Parking is prohibited in front of fire hydrants, hospital entrances, school gates, and emergency exit routes.
